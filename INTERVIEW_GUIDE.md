# C1 English Interview Guide (15 minutes)

## Goal
Assess fluency, accuracy, comprehension, and the ability to argue and react naturally in English.

## Structure

### 0:00–1:00 — Warm-up
- Tell me about yourself.
- What do you do now?

### 1:00–4:00 — Spontaneous speaking
- Describe your current role.
- Tell me about a recent challenge and how you solved it.

### 4:00–7:00 — Opinion and argumentation
- What are the pros and cons of remote work?
- Which side do you agree with more, and why?

### 7:00–10:00 — Hypothetical situation
- Your project is delayed by two weeks. What would you do?
- Who would you inform first?

### 10:00–12:00 — Precision and reformulation
- Rephrase this idea in simpler English: “Effective communication depends on context and audience.”
- Ask for clarification if something is ambiguous.

### 12:00–15:00 — Closing
- What would you improve in your English and why?
- Do you have any questions for me?

## Evaluation criteria
- Fluency
- Grammar
- Vocabulary
- Pronunciation
- Coherence
- Reaction speed

## Final output
- Verdict: C1 / borderline / not C1
- 3 strengths
- 3 weaknesses
- Evidence for each criterion

## C1 validation schema

Use this rubric to score each area from 1 to 5:

- **5** = consistently C1
- **4** = mostly C1, minor slips
- **3** = borderline, needs support
- **2** = below C1, frequent issues
- **1** = clearly below C1

### Criteria
- **Fluency**: can speak smoothly with minimal hesitation
- **Grammar**: controls complex structures with only occasional errors
- **Vocabulary**: uses a broad and precise range of words
- **Pronunciation**: easy to understand without effort
- **Coherence**: ideas are connected and easy to follow
- **Reaction speed**: answers naturally and without long pauses

### Decision rule
- **C1**: mostly 4s and 5s
- **Borderline**: mix of 3s and 4s
- **Not C1**: mostly 1s and 2s

## Audio-ready script

Use this version if you want to record it with pauses:

**Intro**
- “Hi. We are going to do a short English interview to check your C1 level.”
- “Please answer naturally. I may pause between questions.”

**Warm-up**
- “Tell me about yourself.” [pause 3s]
- “What do you do now?” [pause 3s]

**Spontaneous speaking**
- “Describe your current role.” [pause 4s]
- “Tell me about a recent challenge and how you solved it.” [pause 5s]

**Opinion and argumentation**
- “What are the pros and cons of remote work?” [pause 5s]
- “Which side do you agree with more, and why?” [pause 4s]

**Hypothetical situation**
- “Your project is delayed by two weeks. What would you do?” [pause 5s]
- “Who would you inform first?” [pause 3s]

**Precision and reformulation**
- “Rephrase this idea in simpler English: effective communication depends on context and audience.” [pause 5s]
- “If anything is unclear, feel free to ask me for clarification.” [pause 4s]

**Closing**
- “What would you improve in your English and why?” [pause 5s]
- “Do you have any questions for me?” [pause 4s]

**Recording note**
- Leave 3–5 seconds between questions.
- Keep a calm pace.
- Record the answers in sequence.
